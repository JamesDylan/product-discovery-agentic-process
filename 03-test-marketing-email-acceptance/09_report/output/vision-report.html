Marketing email acceptance

We are not fixing a form
we are fixing when we ask

B4B treats every permission ask — starting with marketing email — as a one-time gate cleared at signup, before a user has any reason to trust the product. This vision bets that timing, not wording, is the fix: ask again once the user has proof the product works. The evidence for every claim below sits under it — click to open.

The verdict

B4B treats consent, invitation and expansion as one form step, cleared once at signup and then treated as final. This vision bets that timing beats wording: ask again once a user has proof the product works, not once, cold, before they trust it. Proven on marketing email first, the same mechanism should generalise to every B4B ask with the same shape.

03

The journey, in one view

Three horizons. Each one depends on the one before it holding up — this isn’t a menu.

  1. 01

    Now — the second ask

    A defined engagement trigger (first booking, Nth session) fires a contextual marketing-consent ask, separate from the unchanged signup ask.

  2. 02

    Next — reusable trigger layer

    If timing beats a cold ask, build the trigger logic as infrastructure and point it at bigger gaps: invites, multi-user conversion, corporate email.

  3. 03

    Later — adaptive sequencing

    Once several asks share the layer, sequence which ask fires when, per user — instead of hand-wiring fixed triggers one at a time.

The Now/Next/Later chain, each step’s dependency stated, and what this bet is against.

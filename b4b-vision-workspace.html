The gap

The near-term work is strong. The 12-month picture is missing.

What that costs us

People inside the team don’t believe there is a direction for B4B. A competing internal narrative is generating noise that B4B currently has no answer to.

Who sets it

Senior people in the area they know best — not handed down. This workspace exists so a Product and Design pair can form a point of view and defend it without waiting for permission.

The goal is not a document. The goal is that people believe there is a direction.

A vision that is well argued and changes nobody’s mind has failed.

The trap

The original Accelerator brief only produces the left column. A perfectly run three-day sprint on invite acceptance gives you a feature — and the team still says there is no vision.

If time runs short

Cut prototype polish in step 04, not step 08. Step 08 is not optional and not a nice-to-have at the end. It is the reason this workspace exists.

Click a step

Nine steps. One job each.

You can run them out of order, skip ahead, or loop back. What is fixed: each step leaves a file behind, so the next one has something to stand on. One step’s output/ folder is the next step’s input — and every output is an edit surface. Open it, change what you disagree with, and the next step reads whatever you left there.

The attention trade

Expect a U-curve

Heavy at the start

Setting direction. An hour spent in 01_frame is worth a day spent in 05_pressure-test.

Light through the middle

The work grinds, constrained by both anchors. Don’t stop to re-decide what you already decided.

Heavy again at the end

Deciding whether this is actually right — and turning it into a 12-month arc.

Your first ten minutes

How to actually use it

  1. 1Open the workspace. Read CLAUDE.mdA one-page map, nothing more.
  2. 2Read CONTEXT.mdThe shape of the whole thing, on one screen.
  3. 3Open your problem area folderIts CLAUDE.md says what this run is; its CONTEXT.md holds the step table.
  4. 4Open the step you are on. Read its CONTEXT.mdThat is your brief: inputs, what to do, what to produce, the human check.
  5. 5Work. Save your output into that step’s output/ folderA session that produces only slides or a good feeling has failed.

How to know where things stand

Run the status command in CLAUDE.md. A file in an output/ folder means that step is done. There is no separate tracker to keep updated — the files are the status.

Starting a new problem area

Copy _template to a new numbered folder and fill in its CLAUDE.md. Never edit a live run to change the method — change _template.

Six reasons, in plain terms

Why this works

One job per folder

A step that gathers does not also filter. A step that filters does not also decide. Mixing jobs is how you end up with vague output.

The assistant sees only what the step needs

Each step lists its inputs by exact file path, and says what not to load. An assistant given everything performs worse than one given the right thing.

The order of the work is the order of the folders

No orchestration software, no framework, no tool to learn. Numbered folders carry the sequence; plain text carries the state.

Readable and editable by a person

Plain markdown. No database, no export, no proprietary format. Anyone can open any file, see where things stand, and change it.

Set up the factory once, run it many times

Product context, house view and principles live in _shared. Fix something there and every run improves at the same time.

It survives the person who built it

The judgement is in the files, not in someone’s head. A pair can run a step without waiting on anyone.

Not a local invention — the method is published. ICM (Interpretable Context Methodology), Van Clief & McDermott, arXiv:2603.16021. Every major AI lab has landed on the same pattern: plain folders and files as the way judgement gets handed to a machine.

Read this part properly

Honest limits — of the method

It suits sequential work

With a human checking at each stage. A poor fit for real-time work, many people hitting the same pipeline at once, or a system that needs to branch on its own.

It does not make anyone smarter

It organises thinking. It does not supply it.

Three days is short for real pressure-testing

Step 05 will mostly rely on existing evidence and expert judgement, not new user research. Be honest about that on Friday.

Structure can become theatre. Neatly filled folders can look like progress while the thinking stays shallow.

The files are there to hold judgement, not to replace it. If a step is not helping, say so and collapse it. The constraint is time, not process.

Six rules

Rules that are not negotiable

  1. 1Load only what the step namesDo not point the assistant at the whole folder.
  2. 2One home per factIf something is true in _shared, do not restate it elsewhere. Point at it.
  3. 3Method and live work stay separateChange _template, never a running copy.
  4. 4Every working session ends in a fileWrite the decision down before leaving the room.
  5. 5No polished deck on FridayUse the actual work. A deck signals the artefact cannot carry itself.
  6. 6Step 08_vision-horizon is not optionalIt is the reason this workspace exists.

The measure

How we’ll know it worked

Not by the quality of the documents. By this test:

Can a Product and Design pair run a step and reach a defensible position without the workspace owner in the room?

That is checkable this week. If the answer is no, the process has produced a bottleneck with better filing.

A second test — for the vision itself

Say the one-sentence version to someone who works on B4B but not on this run, and ask them to disagree with it. If they cannot find anything to disagree with, it is not a position yet.

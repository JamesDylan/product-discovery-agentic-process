# Overview

# Product \+ Design Accelerator

## Why

We want to challenge ourselves on **how rapidly Product \+ Design can move together on a real problem when we step outside our normal ways of working**.

This is an opportunity to:

* Rapidly explore and innovate on problems that matter now  
* Work as true Product × Design partners rather than through role boundaries or hand-offs  
* Make full use of existing knowledge, data, patterns, tools and AI  
* Learn what helps us move faster without lowering the quality of our thinking or output

## What

The **Product \+ Design Accelerator** is a focused three-day push to take an agreed problem space and move it to a **tangible, decision-ready Product \+ Design direction**.

**By the end of the three days, each pair should have:**

* A clear view of the problem they are solving  
* A recommended solution direction  
* A tangible artifact to make that direction real — ideally a prototype/design  
* The rationale, key assumptions and trade-offs behind it  
* Enough quality and clarity for the leadership group to make a real decision on what to take forward

The output does **not** need to be pixel-perfect or build-ready, but it does **need to be sufficiently resolved and credible to move immediately into the next step**: technical solution exploration and refinement with senior Engineering partners.

The three-day Accelerator is therefore **not the final finish line**. It is the rapid decision point that gets us onto the path toward the broader goal:

> **By 9 October, we want an agreed and sufficiently resolved solution scope and design direction that Product, Design and Engineering are comfortable planning against.**

*So **the flow** is: 3-day Accelerator → decision-ready Product \+ Design direction → Engineering exploration \+ refinement → plan-ready solution by 9 October.*

## Who

Each problem space will be tackled by a **Product \+ Design pair made up of senior practitioners** from each discipline.

They have been chosen because they bring:

* Strong domain knowledge and context  
* The product and design skills needed to move quickly with good judgement  
* Enough experience to work with ambiguity and make sensible trade-offs  
* The confidence to challenge existing assumptions, role boundaries and ways of working

The pair owns the work jointly. This is not Product defining something and handing it to Design, or Design producing something for Product to review.

The **cross-functional leadership group** ([Lilly Mannerswood](mailto:lilly.mannerswood@serko.com), [Melissa Helyer-Akhara](mailto:melissa.helyer-akhara@serko.com), [Ginger Li](mailto:ginger.li@serko.com)) will:

* Frame the challenge and expected outcome  
* Protect the time and space  
* Remove blockers quickly  
* Be available to consult as SMEs or for fast decisions where needed  
* Give the pairs explicit permission to challenge the status quo (e.g. role and process boundaries)

**Senior Engineering partners will join after** the initial Product \+ Design decision point to explore technical feasibility and help evolve the selected direction.

## How

This is not a formal Design Sprint, and there is no prescribed playbook. We are borrowing useful principles and techniques from Design Sprints, rapid discovery and prototyping, but the method is deliberately flexible.

A few principles:

* **The constraint is time, not process.**  
* Use what we already know. Do not restart discovery from zero.  
* Use AI wherever it genuinely accelerates the work.  
* Reuse existing patterns, research, data and assets.  
* Prototype earlier than feels comfortable.  
* Collapse steps, work in parallel and loop back when you learn something.  
* Make judgement calls rather than waiting for perfect certainty.  
* Be lightweight in how you get there, but **deliberate in what you bring back**.

The Friday (25 Sep) playback is a **decision point, not a showcase**.

# Example timeline & flow

# Example timeline & flow

## Day 0 \- Get ready

Problem spaces and relevant context are available to each pair.

Use this time lightly to make sure you have access to the things you may need \- existing research, Productboard context, analytics, prior designs, customer knowledge, relevant SMEs, design-system assets, etc.

**Do not turn this into an extra unofficial Accelerator day.** The intention is simply to remove avoidable setup friction.

## Day 1 \- Accelerator kickoff

Leadership kickoff to align on:

* Why we are doing this  
* Unpack problem space  
* Desired outcome  
* Shape up the three-day challenge  
* Boundaries we are deliberately removing  
* What “decision-ready” means

Then the pair takes over.

## Day 1 to 3 \- Three-day Accelerator

These are three protected working days.

There is **no required Tuesday / Wednesday / Thursday process**. The activities below are thinking moves we expect you to work through somehow, not sequential stages or daily gates:

**Frame → Explore → Converge → Make tangible → Pressure-test**

You might complete several in a few hours, prototype while still exploring, or revisit the problem framing after something changes your thinking.

#### **What those moves could include**

**Frame**

* Align on what problem you believe you are really solving.  
* Clarify the user/customer outcome and business outcome.  
* Bring together the strongest existing evidence and domain knowledge.  
* Surface important assumptions and unknowns.  
* Decide what matters most to solve within this challenge.

**Explore**

* Generate materially different ways of approaching the problem.  
* Look at existing patterns, analogous experiences and assets you can reuse.  
* Sketch, prompt, prototype or visualise ideas rapidly.  
* Use AI to broaden or challenge the solution space rather than simply polishing the first idea.

**Converge**

* Challenge the options together.  
* Consider customer value, simplicity, business outcome, speed to value and known constraints.  
* Make a call on the strongest direction.  
* Be explicit about why you are choosing it and what trade-offs you are making.

**Make tangible**

* Turn the direction into something people can actually react to.  
* This might be a clickable prototype, considered screen designs, workflow, storyboard or combination of artefacts.  
* Aim for enough quality to clearly communicate the intended experience.  
* It does not need to be pixel-perfect or engineered for production.  
* The key journey, interaction model and important states should be resolved enough for the group to understand how the experience would actually work, rather than only seeing a conceptual direction.

**Pressure-test**

* Challenge the proposed direction using whatever evidence and access is useful and proportionate.  
* This might include existing data, previous research, relevant SMEs, customer-facing colleagues, realistic scenarios or lightweight user feedback where possible.  
* Use AI to critique assumptions or expose edge cases where useful.  
* Refine the direction based on what you learn.  
* Where a technical assumption could materially change the proposed direction, pull in the relevant Engineering or domain expert briefly rather than carrying an avoidable assumption into Friday.

The work does not need to follow this order. **Use whatever sequence gets you to the strongest outcome fastest.**

**Move fast ≠ lower the bar**  
We are deliberately reducing process and ceremony, not the quality of the thinking or the clarity of the output. Scrappy is how you work; it should not describe what you bring back on Friday.

## Day 4 \- Playback \+ decision

Each pair presents its proposed direction to the cross-functional leadership group.

This should use the **actual work**, not require a polished presentation deck.

We want to understand:

* What problem did you ultimately decide you are solving?  
* What informed your thinking?  
* What alternatives did you seriously consider?  
* What direction are you recommending, and why?  
* Show us the proposed experience.  
* What assumptions or risks remain?  
* What now needs to be worked through with Engineering?

**Outcome of the session:** make a clear decision on the Product \+ Design direction we want to take forward.

## Between Day 4 & 8 October \- Technical solution exploration \+ refinement

Bring the selected direction together with senior Engineering partners.

The objective is to:

* Explore feasibility and technical solution options  
* Understand meaningful complexity and constraints  
* Identify anything that materially affects scope  
* Iterate the design where Engineering learning requires it  
* Resolve the remaining Product, Design and Engineering questions needed for planning

This is expected to create some iteration. The Accelerator output is **decision-ready**, not frozen.

## Friday 9 October \- Definition of done

By **9 October, or earlier**, we expect to have:

> **An agreed and sufficiently resolved solution scope and design direction that Product, Design and Engineering are comfortable planning against.**

That is the finish line. The intention is not to allow the work to roll into another open-ended phase of exploration.

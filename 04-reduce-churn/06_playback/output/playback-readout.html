01
The decision requested of leadership

Approve a combined direction — Team Unlock now, and a reframed Trip Pipeline as a parallel, lower-cost validation track — so a plan-ready scope exists by 9 October. This is not a refusal to choose: one direction proceeds on evidence that already triggered it; the other runs its own proof track and can be cut without waiting on the first. We are asking for yes, no, or yes with a changed condition — not thoughts.

See the four conditions, and the fallback if leadership says no
  • A back-test on booking history runs first for B — testing whether historic booking patterns can be reliably detected, not whether a specific upcoming trip can be predicted.
  • The churn effect for both B and D is proven only by a 50/50 A/B test, assigned per organisation.
  • The GDPR and opt-in check is done before any A/B test starts on real European users.
  • James owns closing the boundary question with Anna Bondarenko's experiment before D ships.

If leadership says no: proceed on Team Unlock alone — its reversal condition is independently met and doesn't depend on Trip Pipeline. Park the reframed Trip Pipeline as a lower-cost follow-on once Team Unlock ships and the Anna Bondarenko boundary is closed.

The direction was contested — here's how it resolved

01

Team Unlock proceeds

The reversal condition set in 03_converge — orgs with invited, self-booking travellers churn materially less — was confirmed in 05: “orgs with invited travellers churn less… going from 1 user to 2 users is one of the best predictors of improved retention.” Its open kill reasons from 03 still stand and aren't waived: the invite/self-book mechanism already ships and churn persists on its own; its adoption message risks cutting arranger sessions. James owns closing the boundary question with Anna Bondarenko's experiment before it ships.

Triggers 03's own pre-set condition · unresolved: adoption message, Anna Bondarenko boundary

02

Trip Pipeline proceeds, reframed

Back-test math shows only ~23% of B4B companies book at least once every 30 days, so trip-prediction rarely has a clean target to hit. Reframed from predicting the arranger's next trip to surfacing booking-timing and policy suggestions from the org's own historic patterns — pattern detection, not prediction. Runs on its own validation track: it can be folded in or dropped on its own evidence, independent of Team Unlock.

Removes 05's main pressure-test weakness · own back-test, no kill threshold set yet — left as an open risk for the room

03

The trade-off, stated plainly

“We are recommending two directions at once rather than picking one, which costs focus and Engineering bandwidth up front. In exchange we do not bet the whole recommendation on a single mechanism: Team Unlock is the direction the evidence already points to; Trip Pipeline is a cheaper, pattern-based validation track that can be tested in parallel and folded in or dropped based on its own back-test, without waiting on Team Unlock to prove itself first.”

Reach vs. focus · the stated cost of not choosing only one

See the two converge passes, and why Team Unlock moved from killed to combined

First pass — rejected at human check. Chose Disruption Radar over Traveller Signal Loop, Autopilot and Digest, Trip Health Score and Change-Request Approvals. James rejected the option set outright: disruption frequency is too low to bring arrangers back. The lesson carried into pass two — business outcome now counts trigger frequency; Disruption Radar had scored a 4 there, which was wrong.

Second pass, on the v2 option set — scores out of 25: Trip Pipeline 18, Disruption Radar 16, Team Unlock 14, Autopilot and Digest 14, Traveller Roster 12, Rate Lock 11, Booker Rewards 8. The margin is small — the eliminations carried the call, not the scores. Team Unlock's score reflected a “killed” verdict at scoring time; that call changed once 05 confirmed its reversal condition was met. It is now combined with Trip Pipeline, not eliminated.

See the limits on this evidence
  • It is correlation. No evidence separates “no session caused churn” from “no travel caused both.” The A/B test is the only test of cause.
  • The 30-day curve is measured across the entire base. The multi-user cut is assumed: “we can assume the same applies to the 30 day window.”

  • The 41.61% figure is “at least once in 12 months,” not a typical 30-day window — do not present it as a monthly reach number.

05

What the arranger sees

An annotated wireframe storyboard, not a clickable prototype — Figma access wasn't authenticated for this run.

Frame 1 · Day 1

First view of booking-pattern suggestions

Two or three suggestions derived from the org's own booking history, each with a one-line reason — “you usually book this route monthly,” “you typically book the day before travel.” Capped for large orgs. An empty-state variant sits beside it. Visible to arrangers and admins only.

Carries:
The cold-start problem is solved without needing to predict a specific trip — the pattern is legible enough to trust.
Frame 2 · Week 2–6

The signal, inside Bookings and Trips

A generic, loss-framed nudge tied to a specific booking pattern — “prices tend to rise if you book less than 3 weeks out” — no dollar-specific claim. Visually distinct from the account's other notices.

Carries:
A concrete, personal reason to come back — a pattern-based nudge, not a trip prediction.
Frame 3 · Day 200

Messy, at scale

Many suggestions and signals at once, mixed sources, mixed stages — unconfirmed, confirmed, signalled, booked, dismissed.

Carries:
A sustained loop at real scale, not a first-run novelty.

Deliberately grey: the pattern-detection logic itself, the price-watching mechanism, and the message schedule across the existing churn clocks. Updated to match 05, before Friday: visibility restricted to arrangers and admins, a suggestion cap for large orgs, loss framing on the signal, and the historic-pattern mechanism replacing trip prediction — all four reflected above and in artefact-notes.md.

What this looks like in 12 months

Draft, sketched from 03's horizon section — 08_vision-horizon builds this properly.

The horizon — draft

B4B spreads travel ownership across the org, and helps every arranger get ahead of travel rather than only clean up after it.

Team Unlock is the load-bearing 12-month story — value spreads across the org as more people book, less concentration of churn risk in one arranger. Trip Pipeline, reframed as pattern-based suggestions, is the near-term proof point underneath it. Rate Lock becomes possible once pattern data matures into real trip visibility. One surface, more signals: disruptions and policy exceptions join once policy controls ship. Boundaries held: no spend reporting, no org-level forecasting.

Falsifiable: orgs may not want to widen who books, or arrangers may not act on pattern-based suggestions. Not covered: the account that goes dark when its one arranger leaves and never invites anyone — that sits with the separate invoicing and expenses track.

§

Read with care

Prepared before the session, not after the question lands.

  1. The hard question

    “Your own reversal condition for Team Unlock was met. Why are you still recommending Trip Pipeline too?” Answer: Team Unlock proceeds on its own trigger; Trip Pipeline runs a separate, cheaper validation track that doesn't block it and can be cut on its own evidence. This is sequencing, not a refusal to decide.

  2. Where we might present activity as evidence

    Seven options, two converge passes and a scoring table are activity. The scores are close (18 vs 16) — the eliminations, and now Team Unlock's reversal, carry the call, not the score.

  3. The storyboard tests the story, not the mechanism

    It shows the experience works if pattern detection works. It does not test pattern detection — only the back-test and the A/B test do.

  4. 41.61% sounds like reach. It isn't monthly reach.

    It's “at least once in 12 months.” Presenting it as a typical-month number overstates how often this lever fires.

  5. The message-schedule risk has no stated reason

    “Accept the risk and ship anyway” — with no reason given. Leadership may reasonably ask for one before approving.

Lilly's objection

“You have never built prediction, or now pattern detection either. Why should we believe it works?” The honest answer is the back-test plus the A/B test, not a claim.

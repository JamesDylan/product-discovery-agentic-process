# What a good 12-month vision looks like here

## The problem this project exists to solve

The team does not believe there is a B4B vision. The Serko AI narrative is generating noise that
B4B has no answer to. Near-term execution is strong; the horizon is absent.

A vision that does not change what the team believes has failed, regardless of its quality.

## Tests a vision must pass

1. **Falsifiable.** It could be wrong. If no one could disagree with it, it says nothing.
2. **Sequenced.** It says what comes first, second, third — and why that order.
3. **Ownable.** A senior practitioner can see their sphere of influence in it and act without asking permission.
4. **Legible in one sentence.** If it needs a deck to land, it isn't a vision.
5. **Connected to today.** Named bridge from current product state to the 12-month state.
6. **Survives the "so what" test.** Passes with a customer, an engineer, and Booking.com.

## Failure modes to name out loud

- **Feature list in a trenchcoat.** A roadmap relabelled as vision.
- **Vision by aggregation.** Stapling two workstream outputs together and calling it a strategy.
- **Aspiration with no mechanism.** "AI-powered X" with no account of how it becomes true.
- **Consensus mush.** Every stakeholder's concern included, so nothing is chosen.
- **Orphaned vision.** Correct and unowned. Nobody acts differently on Monday.

## The authorship principle

From the brief and the meeting context: vision is set by senior leaders in their sphere of
influence, not dictated from the top. The pipeline must produce *points of view*, not specs.
When a step starts producing specs, flag it.
